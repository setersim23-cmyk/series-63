How this book is coded
This is not a book you read straight through and hope. It is a diagnostic instrument. Every statement in it carries a tag
telling you what kind of knowledge it is — and therefore what to do when you get it wrong.
◆ 	CONCEPT 	Understand the logic. If you miss one of these, re-derive it — explain it out loud
without the book. Re-reading will not help.
● 	MEMORIZE 	A raw fact, number, or list. Understanding will not help; repetition will. Every one of
these is repeated on the memorize sheet at the end of its chapter.
▲ 	TRAP 	Where the exam attacks this material. Usually the wrong answer that feels right. Read
these last before you walk in.
→ 	LINK 	This cell depends on, or feeds, another cell. Weakness here may actually be weakness
there.
The distinction matters more than it sounds. When you miss a question, the useful first move is not “review that
chapter.” It is: was the thing I missed a concept or a fact? If you miss a ◆ concept item, re-reading will not fix it — you
have to re-derive the logic until you can explain it out loud without the book. If you miss a ● memorize item,
understanding will not fix it — you need repetition. Treating the two the same way is the single most common reason
people plateau at 68% on this exam.
Roughly 60% of what follows is tagged ◆. That is the good news. The Series 63 is not a memorization exam wearing a
law costume; it is a vocabulary and logic exam with about ninety hard facts bolted on. The ● items are collected again at
the back of the book in a single sheet so you can drill them without re-reading anything.
The cell codes and your Harada chart
The eight subject areas map onto the eight surrounding blocks of a Mandal chart, and each subject breaks into exactly
eight cells. That gives you sixty-four cells with stable addresses — BDR-4, ETH-7, REM-2 — that you can write straight
onto the grid.
Work it this way. Read a chapter. At the end of each cell, mark the cell on your chart with one of three states: solid,
shaky, or blank. Then — and this is the part that makes the chart earn its keep — for every cell you mark shaky or blank,
note whether the weakness is on the ◆ items or the ● items. Shaky-on-concepts and shaky-on-facts are different
diseases with different treatments, and the chart is where you keep that diagnosis visible.

Cross-references, and how to use them

One orienting idea before you start
The shape of the whole subject
Every state securities act does four things in sequence. It defines what counts as a security. It requires
that securities, and the people who sell or advise on them, be registered — subject to a list of escape
hatches. It forbids a catalogue of specific dishonest conduct. And it gives one official, the Administrator,
the tools to enforce all of that, backed by private lawsuits and criminal prosecution. Definitions,
registration, conduct, enforcement. Every question on this exam is somewhere in that sequence, and
knowing which of the four you are in narrows the answer set before you read the choices.
A note on your own background, because it will help more than you expect. You already work with the products this
exam legislates around: index-linked structured products, variable annuity hedging, separate accounts, institutional
counterparties. That gives you real intuition about which side of the fixed/variable line a product sits on, what an
institutional buyer looks like, and why disclosure obligations attach where they do. Where that intuition maps onto an
exam concept below, it is called out. Where it does not — and there are a few places where the state-law answer is
deliberately cruder than market reality — that is called out too, because your professional instinct will occasionally
point at the wrong answer.

SEC 	Regulation of Securities and Issuers
9% · 5 questions
THE SPINE
Nothing in the Act switches on until the thing being sold is a security. Answer that question, then ask
whether registration is required, then ask by which method. Three filters, always in that order. Only
five questions come from this chapter, but the definitions you build here are quoted back at you in
all seven of the others.
SEC connector map — the three-filter funnel.
SEC-1 	Definition of a security, and the Howey test
ALSO IN
Achievable Ch. 1 Definitions — Issuers & securities (Howey test)
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 1 · pp. 1–3
STC 43e Ch. 1 Key Terms — Security, p. 6; Ch. 2 Securities, p. 16
◆ 	The Act defines “security” with a long list of named instruments — stock, treasury stock, bonds,
debentures, notes, evidences of indebtedness, collateral trust certificates, transferable shares, voting
trust certificates, preorganization subscriptions, certificates of interest in oil, gas or mining titles,

-- 5 of 86 --

warrants and rights to subscribe — and then closes with a catch-all for anything commonly known as
a security. The list is deliberately open-ended. That design choice is the whole point: substance
governs, not the label on the paperwork.
◆ 	Because the list cannot anticipate every arrangement, the catch-all carries the term “investment
contract,” and the Supreme Court supplied the four-part test for what an investment contract is. All
four prongs must be present: an investment of money, in a common enterprise, with an expectation
of profits, derived primarily from the efforts of others.
● 	The four Howey prongs, in order: money in · common enterprise · expectation of profit · profits from
the efforts of others.
◆ 	The fourth prong is where most novel arrangements are decided. If the investor does the work — a
person who buys a franchise and runs it themselves — there is no security. If a promoter does the
work and the investor waits for a check, there usually is one. Orange groves paired with a cultivation
contract, working interests in an oil well managed by the operator, fractional interests in racehorses
managed by a trainer, and tokens sold with a promise that the issuing team will build the network all
come out the same way.
▲ 	“It is real estate, so it cannot be a security.” Wrong. Real estate sold with a management contract that
produces passive income for the buyer is the original Howey fact pattern.
→ 	Feeds SEC-2 (what is excluded), SEC-4 and SEC-5 (exemptions apply only once something IS a
security), and REM-2 (the Administrator’s jurisdiction attaches to offers and sales of securities).
SEC-2 	What is not a security
ALSO IN
Achievable Ch. 1 Definitions — Securities (what is excluded)
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 1 · pp. 2–3
STC 43e Ch. 2 Securities, pp. 16–20
◆ 	A short list of product families sits outside the definition, and the organizing idea is who bears the
investment risk. If the issuer or insurer guarantees the outcome and absorbs the market risk, it is not
a security. If the buyer absorbs the market risk, it is.
● 	Not securities: fixed annuities and fixed insurance products · whole life and endowment policies ·
commodity futures contracts · physical commodities and precious metals held directly · collectibles,
art, rare coins · bank certificates of deposit, savings accounts, demand deposits · real estate owned
directly for personal use or investment without a management arrangement · retirement plan
interests such as IRAs and 401(k) accounts themselves (the investments inside them are another
matter).

-- 6 of 86 --

◆ 	Variable products are securities. In a variable annuity or variable life contract the contract holder’s
account value moves with a separate account portfolio, so the holder bears the investment risk. You
know this cold from the hedging side of your work: a fixed annuity leaves the insurer holding the
market exposure, which is why the carrier hedges it on its own balance sheet; a variable contract
passes that exposure through to the policyholder, which is why the separate account is registered.
● 	Selling a variable product requires a securities registration (Series 6 or 7 plus the state exam) AND a
state insurance license. Selling a fixed annuity requires only the insurance license.
◆ 	Equity-indexed and index-linked annuities are the genuinely contested middle. Where the contract
guarantees principal and credits a formula-driven return, most states treat it as insurance; where the
buyer takes real downside, it looks like a security. The exam avoids the hard cases — if a question
says “fixed” it means not a security, and if it says “variable” it means security.
▲ 	“A fixed annuity is a security because the return depends on an investment portfolio.” Wrong — the
insurer guarantees the rate and holds the risk. This is described as the single most recycled
distinction in this chapter, and it is worth being able to answer without pausing.
▲ 	“A bank CD is never a security.” Mostly true and it is the exam answer — but know the edge: a long-
dated brokered CD with unusual features that trades in a secondary market can be recharacterized. If
the stem says “certificate of deposit at an insured bank,” answer not a security.
SEC-3 	Offer, sale, and the events that are neither
ALSO IN
Achievable Ch. 1 Definitions — Transactions
$2 Textbook Mod 4, Lesson 3 · p. 74
STC 43e Ch. 7 Offers and Sales, p. 135; Gifts of Securities, p. 139
⚠ 2DT parks these in Module 4 and never defines “guaranteed” at all.
◆ 	The Act does not regulate securities in the abstract; it regulates offers and sales of them. A sale is any
contract of sale, contract to sell, or disposition of a security for value. An offer is any attempt to
dispose of a security, or any solicitation of an offer to buy, for value. The operative phrase in both is
“for value.” No value moving, no sale.
◆ 	Note how far “offer” reaches: an attempt counts. The pitch that fails is still an offer, and it is still
within the Administrator’s jurisdiction and still subject to the antifraud rules.
● 	These ARE sales even though they look like gifts or non-events: a gift of ASSESSABLE stock (the
recipient takes on a future obligation, which is value) · any security given as a bonus with the
purchase of anything else, security or not · a warrant or right delivered together with a bond.

-- 7 of 86 --

● 	The sale of a warrant, right, or convertible security is also an OFFER of the underlying security it can
be converted into or used to buy.
● 	These are NOT sales: a bona fide gift of non-assessable stock · a bona fide pledge or loan of securities
as collateral · a stock dividend or stock split where the holder gives nothing · a merger, consolidation,
reclassification, or sale of assets approved by a vote of the security holders · an exchange of securities
in a judicially approved reorganization.
◆ 	Keep “not a sale” and “exempt transaction” in separate drawers. An exempt transaction IS a sale that
happens to skip registration. An excluded event is not a sale at all, so no registration analysis ever
begins.
▲ 	“The stock was free, so no sale occurred.” Wrong when the free security rides along with a purchase
— part of the price is attributed to it, and both instruments were sold.
SEC-4 	Exempt securities — exempt because of what they are
ALSO IN
Achievable 2.5 Securities
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 2 · pp. 4–6
STC 43e Ch. 2 Exempt Securities, p. 27
◆ 	An exempt security is exempt from REGISTRATION because of the nature of the instrument or its
issuer. The status attaches to the security and travels with it into every subsequent transaction,
forever. Once exempt, always exempt.
● 	The list: U.S. government and agency securities · Canadian federal and provincial government
securities · securities of foreign governments with which the U.S. maintains diplomatic relations ·
municipal securities of any state or political subdivision · securities issued by banks, savings
institutions, and trust companies · securities issued by insurance companies · securities of federally
regulated public utilities and common carriers · securities of nonprofit religious, educational,
charitable, fraternal, and social organizations · commercial paper · securities issued in connection
with employee benefit plans · securities of federal covered status.
● 	Commercial paper qualifies only if all three conditions hold: maturity of nine months or less,
denominations of at least $50,000, and a rating in one of the three highest categories from a
nationally recognized rating organization.
▲ 	Bank securities means the BANK. A bank HOLDING COMPANY’s stock is not exempt. Any time a stem
inserts the words “holding company,” the exemption is gone.
▲ 	Insurance company securities are exempt — meaning the insurer’s own stock and bonds. The
variable annuity and variable life contracts the insurer sells are separate-account securities and are

-- 8 of 86 --

NOT covered by that exemption. Two different things issued by the same company, two different
answers.
◆ 	Notice what unites the list: issuers already supervised by another regulator, or governments. The
theory is that a second layer of state merit review adds nothing. That is also why the exemption never
touches fraud — see SEC-6.
SEC-5 	Exempt transactions — exempt because of how the sale happens
ALSO IN
Achievable 2.5 Securities
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 2 · pp. 4–6
STC 43e Ch. 2 Exempt Transactions, p. 31
◆ 	A transaction exemption looks at the circumstances of a particular trade rather than at the
instrument. It is single-use. It covers the transaction described and nothing after it; the next trade in
the same security has to find its own exemption or register.
● 	The heavily tested ones: an isolated non-issuer transaction · any transaction with an institutional
investor · a private placement directed to no more than ten non-institutional persons in the state in
twelve consecutive months · a preorganization certificate offered to no more than ten subscribers · an
unsolicited order received from a customer · a transaction by a fiduciary — executor, administrator,
receiver, trustee in bankruptcy, sheriff, marshal, conservator, guardian · a non-issuer transaction in a
security listed in a recognized securities manual · an offer or sale to existing security holders where
no commission is paid.
● 	The private placement exemption carries two conditions beyond the count: the seller must
reasonably believe every non-institutional buyer is purchasing for investment rather than resale, and
no commission or other remuneration may be paid for soliciting a non-institutional buyer.
● 	The preorganization exemption carries its own two: no payment may be received from any
subscriber, and no commission may be paid for soliciting them.
◆ 	Institutional investors are counted separately and without limit. The ten-person cap governs only
non-institutional persons. Ten retail persons plus fifty institutions is fine. Your world lives almost
entirely inside this exemption — an offering placed with insurance carriers and pension funds does
not touch the ten-person cap at all.
▲ 	“The eleventh institution defeats the private placement exemption.” Wrong — institutions do not
count.
▲ 	“Because the first sale was exempt, later sales of the same security are exempt.” Wrong — that is the
exempt-SECURITY rule, not the exempt-TRANSACTION rule. This swap is the most common way this
chapter is tested.

◆ 	Compare with federal law once and move on: the state private placement exemption counts ten or
fewer non-institutional persons; the federal Regulation D Rule 506 exemption counts thirty-five or
fewer non-accredited investors. Because Rule 506 securities became federal covered, the state
exemption is now largely vestigial in practice — but it is still fully testable.
SEC-6 	Federal covered securities, notice filing, and the antifraud floor
ALSO IN
Achievable 2.5 Securities; Ch. 1 Definitions — Exempt & excluded
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 2 · pp. 5–6
STC 43e Ch. 2 Federal Exemptions, p. 35; Anti-Fraud Provisions, p. 37
⚠ 2DT implies all four federal covered categories notice-file. STC p. 35 is the corrective.
◆ 	In 1996 Congress redrew the federal-state line. For certain categories, states are preempted from
requiring registration at all. Those are federal covered securities. What states retain is narrow and
specific: a notice filing with a fee, and full antifraud authority.
● 	Four categories of federal covered security: securities listed on a national exchange (and any security
of equal or senior rank to a listed one) · securities issued by registered investment companies ·
securities sold to qualified purchasers · securities sold under Regulation D Rule 506.
● 	Only two of the four are actually required to notice file: investment company securities, and Rule 506
offerings. Issuers of exchange-listed securities and of securities sold to qualified purchasers do not
notice file.
● 	A notice filing consists of: a statement identifying the issuer and the security, copies of what was filed
with the SEC (or where to get them), a consent to service of process, and the state fee. For mutual
funds it is typically renewed annually.
◆ 	What the state can still do with a federal covered security: refuse a deficient or unpaid filing,
investigate and prosecute fraud, and suspend transactions where fraud is found. What it cannot do:
review the merits, require registration by coordination or qualification, or impose disclosure
obligations beyond the federal ones.
▲ 	Rule 506 is federal covered. Rule 504 is not. (Rule 505 was repealed in 2016 and should not appear
— if it does, it is a distractor.)
◆ 	The one rule with no exceptions anywhere in this subject: nothing is ever exempt from the antifraud
provisions. Not an exempt security, not an exempt transaction, not a federal covered security, not any
person. Exemption relieves the duty to register. It never relieves the duty to deal honestly. When a
stem asks which of four items is exempt from the antifraud provisions, the answer is none of them.

SEC-7 	The three registration methods
ALSO IN
Achievable 2.5 Securities
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 3 · pp. 7–9
STC 43e Ch. 2 Types of State Securities Registration, p. 23
◆ 	A security that is neither federal covered nor exempt must register in every state where it is offered.
There are three doors, and which one an issuer uses is determined by its federal status, not by
preference.
● 	Registration by FILING (also called notification) — the streamlined path for seasoned issuers with an
established earnings history. Rarely used today.
● 	Registration by COORDINATION — for a security registering with the SEC at the same time under the
Securities Act of 1933. This is the common path for a public offering.
● 	Registration by QUALIFICATION — the fallback for everything else, including intrastate offerings that
never go to the SEC. The most demanding: full disclosure of the issuer, its capital structure, its officers
and directors, the use of proceeds, audited financials, and an opinion of counsel that the security is
legally issued, fully paid and non-assessable.
● 	Coordination becomes effective automatically and simultaneously with the federal registration,
provided no stop order is in effect, the registration statement has been on file with the
ADMINISTRATOR for the required period, and a statement of the maximum and minimum proposed
offering prices and maximum underwriting discounts has been on file with the Administrator for two
full business days.
● 	Qualification becomes effective only when the Administrator affirmatively declares it effective. There
is no automatic mechanism.
● 	Every method requires, in addition to the substantive filing: a consent to service of process, the filing
fee, and whatever information about the issuer, the offering, and the underwriters the Administrator
requires. The Administrator may also require an escrow or an impound of proceeds, and may require
sales to be made through a specified escrow agent.
▲ 	The ten-day clock in coordination runs with the state Administrator, not the SEC. A stem that says “on
file with the SEC for ten days” is describing the wrong filing.
▲ 	“Qualification becomes effective automatically after thirty days.” Wrong — that is the PERSON
registration clock (see BDR-5). Securities registered by qualification wait for the Administrator to act.
SEC-8 	Life of a securities registration, and stop orders

-- 11 of 86 --

ALSO IN
Achievable 2.5 Securities; Ch. 3 Enforcement
$2 Textbook Mod 1, Lesson 3 · pp. 9–10
STC 43e Ch. 2 Registering Securities, pp. 21–22; Denial, Suspension, Revocation, p. 26
● 	A securities registration is effective for one year from its effective date. This is the only registration
under the Act that does not expire on December 31 — persons all expire at calendar year end. Expect
that contrast to be tested.
● 	The registration statement may be filed by the issuer, by any other person on whose behalf the
offering is being made, or by a registered broker-dealer.
◆ 	The Administrator’s kill switch is the stop order, which denies, suspends, or revokes the effectiveness
of a registration statement. It requires grounds, and the grounds are enumerated.
● 	Grounds for a stop order include: the registration statement is incomplete or contains a material
misstatement or omission · the offering violates the Act or an order · the issuer’s enterprise or
method of business is illegal · the offering would work a fraud on purchasers · underwriter
compensation or promoters’ profits are unreasonable · the applicable filing fee was not paid · the
security is the subject of an injunction by a court.
● 	Due process: a stop order may not be entered without prior written notice to the applicant and every
person on record, an opportunity for a hearing, and written findings of fact and conclusions of law.
The Administrator MAY summarily postpone or suspend effectiveness pending final determination,
but must then promptly notify the parties and hold a hearing on request — within fifteen days of a
written request.
◆ 	The Administrator may not enter a stop order based on facts known when the registration became
effective unless the proceeding is instituted within thirty days after effectiveness. The Act does not let
a regulator sit on knowledge and then pull the rug.
● 	A stop order may be modified or vacated by the Administrator at any time if conditions change.

◆ 	An investment adviser is a person who, for compensation, engages in the business of advising others
as to the value of securities or the advisability of investing in, purchasing, or selling securities — or
who, as part of a regular business, issues analyses or reports concerning securities. Collapse that into
three prongs.
● 	A — Advice about SECURITIES. B — in the BUSINESS of giving it. C — for COMPENSATION. All three
must be present. Any one missing and the person is not an adviser.
◆ 	Each prong has a live edge. Advice must be about securities, so generic budgeting, insurance-only
advice, commodities, and direct real estate all fail prong A. Being in the business means holding out as
an adviser or advising regularly — an occasional favor fails prong B. Compensation need not be cash,
need not be labeled an advisory fee, and need not come from the client; anything of economic value
satisfies prong C. A firm that advises another firm in exchange for legal services has been
compensated.
▲ 	“The adviser was not paid separately for the advice, so there is no compensation.” Careful — indirect
and bundled compensation counts. This is exactly how a wrap fee converts a broker-dealer into an
adviser (see ETH-8).
◆ 	The exam’s favorite target is prong A. Someone advising exclusively on fixed annuities, whole life, or
physical gold is not giving advice about securities, so no amount of business regularity or
compensation makes them an adviser.

COM-3 	Performance guarantees
ALSO IN

◆ 	No representation may guarantee future investment performance or protect against loss. The rule

● 	Prohibited: promising a rate of return · promising outperformance · saying the client cannot lose

● 	The state antifraud provision makes it unlawful, in connection with the offer, sale, OR PURCHASE of

◆ 	Manipulation is lying to the market with orders instead of words — creating a false impression of

● 	SPOOFING — entering bids or offers with the intent to cancel before execution. Tell: intent to cancel.
● 	LAYERING — spoofing at multiple price levels with a series of non-bona-fide orders. Tell: multiple

● 	FRONT-RUNNING — trading ahead of a known pending customer order to profit from the price move

● 	WASH SALES and MATCHED ORDERS — transactions producing no real change in beneficial

● 	PUMP AND DUMP — spreading false favorable information to inflate a price, then selling into the
demand created. Tell: promotion followed by the promoter selling.
● 	The publication side: a firm may not publish or circulate, or cause to be circulated, any
communication reporting a transaction as a purchase or sale unless it believes the trade was bona
fide, or quoting a bid or offer unless it believes the quotation is bona fide. Tell: publicizing prices or

▲ 	“The tipper did not trade, so the tipper is not liable.” Wrong — tipping is itself a violation.

ordinarily requires no disclosure to the client unless it increases the client’s cost.

information in order to execute transactions that would otherwise be prohibited. The classic patterns are
inflating a customer’s stated net worth to qualify the account for margin or options, and overstating
investment experience to clear a suitability screen. The customer’s consent does not cure it; the false
record is the violation.

Prior WRITTEN NOTICE to the firm is required in all cases regardless of compensation; where
compensation is involved the firm must also approve and supervise. The transaction need not involve a
firm customer to be a violation.
ETH-8 	VULNERABLE ADULT — an individual 65 or older, or an adult protected under the state’s adult protective
services statute.
ETH-8 	TRUSTED CONTACT — firms must make reasonable efforts to obtain the name of a trusted contact
person when opening an account. The client may decline. The trusted contact has NO authority over the
account; they are an information resource the firm may reach if it suspects exploitation, suspects
diminished capacity, or cannot reach the client.
ETH-8 	TEMPORARY HOLDS — a firm that reasonably believes financial exploitation is occurring, has occurred,
or is being attempted may place a temporary hold on a DISBURSEMENT. The initial hold runs up to 15
BUSINESS DAYS and may be extended by 10 more, for 25 total, if the firm’s internal review supports it.
Notice must go to the Administrator and to adult protective services within 2 business days, and the firm
must conduct an internal review.
ETH-8 	Red flags: sudden unexplained changes in financial patterns · a new friend or caregiver directing

ETH-8 	SOFT DOLLARS — an adviser directing client brokerage commissions to a broker in exchange for
research and services falls within a safe harbor if the services benefit the clients whose commissions are
used. ELIGIBLE: research reports, financial databases and market data, analytical and portfolio-
management software, seminars on research topics. NOT ELIGIBLE: office rent, furniture, computer
hardware for general use, travel, entertainment, telephone bills, salaries. If it would exist without the
investment process, it is overhead. Best execution is still required, and the arrangements must be
disclosed.
ETH-8 	AGENCY CROSS TRANSACTION — the adviser acts as agent for both buyer and seller. Permitted only with

transactions and total compensation. The condition no paperwork waives: the adviser may not have
RECOMMENDED the transaction to both parties.
ETH-8 	WRAP FEE PROGRAM — a program under which a client pays a single fee, not based directly on
transactions, covering both advisory services and execution. The sponsor is an investment adviser and
must register. A broker-dealer charging a wrap fee has received special compensation for advice and
loses its exclusion from the adviser definition. Wrap clients receive a special wrap-program brochure

does not vary with trading, the incentive is to trade too LITTLE.

REM 	Remedies and Administrative Provisions

THE SPINE
One violation opens three separate doors: an administrative action by the Administrator, a private
lawsuit by the buyer, and a criminal prosecution by a prosecutor. Different actors, different
standards of proof, different clocks. Most of the questions here are really asking which door you are
standing in — and the most reliable single question type is what the Administrator can and cannot
do without a court.
REM connector map — one violation, three tracks.
REM-1 	The Administrator — powers and limits

ALSO IN
Achievable Ch. 3 Enforcement

STC 43e Ch. 7 Powers of the State Administrator, p. 143; Injunctions, p. 144
◆ 	The Administrator is the state’s senior securities official. Titles vary — Securities Commissioner,
Director of the Division of Securities, Secretary of State — and the office is usually a staff rather than a
person. The powers and constraints are the same regardless of the label.
● 	The Administrator CAN: make, amend, and rescind rules and forms · issue interpretive and exemptive
orders · register and deny, suspend, revoke, cancel, or condition registrations · conduct investigations
inside or outside the state · subpoena witnesses and documents · administer oaths and take
depositions · examine registrants’ books and records · issue cease-and-desist orders · issue stop
orders against securities registrations · impose administrative fines and require restitution · refer
matters for criminal prosecution · apply to a court for an injunction or the appointment of a receiver ·
cooperate with other administrators and with federal regulators.
● 	The Administrator CANNOT: make arrests · prosecute crimes · impose criminal fines or
imprisonment · issue an injunction · hold a person in contempt · grant immunity to a witness. Every
item on that list is a judicial function requiring a court.
◆ 	The distinction is worth internalizing as a single sentence: the Administrator is a regulator with
subpoena power, not a judge and not a prosecutor. Where the Act needs judicial force, the
Administrator goes to court and asks for it.
● 	Rulemaking limits: a rule or order may be adopted only if it is in the public interest and consistent
with the purposes of the Act, and no rule may be inconsistent with federal law where federal law
preempts. No provision imposing liability applies to an act done in good faith reliance on a rule or
order, even if that rule is later amended or held invalid.
▲ 	“The Administrator issued an injunction against the firm.” Wrong every time — the Administrator
issues cease-and-desist orders and asks a court for injunctions.

◆ 	A state’s securities law reaches a transaction if any one of three contacts occurs in that state. Any
single one is sufficient, which means a transaction routinely falls under two states’ laws at once.

● 	The three triggers: an offer to SELL or to BUY is made in the state · an offer is ACCEPTED in the state ·

state is generally made there, unless the offer carries an effective legend limiting it and no follow-up
is directed into that state.
▲ 	“Jurisdiction requires the buyer and seller to be in the same state.” Wrong — one contact is enough,
and overlapping jurisdiction is normal.
▲ 	The two-thirds circulation test is a favorite. A magazine published in the state with 70% of its

◆ 	A CEASE-AND-DESIST order directs a person to stop conduct that violates the Act. It may issue

ALSO IN
Achievable Ch. 3 Enforcement
$2 Textbook Mod 4, Lesson 1 · pp. 68–69
STC 43e Ch. 7 Administrative Actions Associated with Registration, p. 141; Cancellations and Withdrawals, p. 145
◆ 	Before any punitive final action against a registration — denial, suspension, revocation, or the
imposition of conditions — three things must happen, and the exam tests the sequence.

● 	The three steps: PRIOR WRITTEN NOTICE stating the grounds and the proposed action · an
OPPORTUNITY FOR A HEARING, held within fifteen days of a written request · WRITTEN FINDINGS
of fact and conclusions of law.
◆ 	Two independent requirements must both be satisfied for a punitive action: there must be statutory
cause — a ground listed in the Act — AND the action must be in the public interest. Cause alone is not
enough, which is why an ancient minor infraction generally does not support revocation.
◆ 	Non-punitive terminations bypass the machinery because nothing is being alleged. CANCELLATION
applies where a registrant no longer exists, has ceased doing business, cannot be located after
reasonable search, or is mentally incompetent. WITHDRAWAL is the registrant’s own voluntary exit.
▲ 	A stem describing mail returned undeliverable, a dissolved entity, or an incapacitated registrant is

suspension, or revocation.
● 	The Administrator may also impose lesser sanctions — censure, a bar or suspension from
association, administrative fines, and restitution — without revoking a registration.
REM-6 	Civil liability
ALSO IN
Achievable Ch. 3 Enforcement
$2 Textbook Mod 4, Lesson 2 · pp. 70–72
STC 43e Ch. 7 Civil Liability for Securities Sold in Violation of the Act, p. 146; Civil Liability for Fraudulent Investment Advice, p.
148
⚠ STC p. 147 states the limitations period as three years from the violation or two from discovery, whichever is first — the same
as this cell.
◆ 	The Act gives a defrauded or improperly sold BUYER a private right of action against the seller. It is
the most powerful remedy in private hands because the buyer need not wait for any regulator to act.
● 	Conduct creating civil liability: selling an unregistered, non-exempt security · selling through an
unregistered broker-dealer or agent · selling by means of an untrue statement of material fact or a
material omission · selling in violation of a rule or order.
● 	SCIENTER IS NOT REQUIRED. An innocent or merely negligent violation of the registration or sales-
conduct rules creates liability. The buyer proves the violation and the purchase; intent is not an
element.
● 	RESCISSION formula — the buyer recovers: the price paid, PLUS interest from the date of purchase,
PLUS court costs and reasonable attorney fees, MINUS any income received from the security. The
buyer must TENDER the security back.

-- 78 of 86 --

● 	DAMAGES formula — where the buyer has already sold and cannot tender: the same calculation,
MINUS the proceeds of that sale. No tender is required.
● 	RESCISSION OFFER — a seller who discovers a violation may make a written offer to unwind the
transaction on the same terms a court would order. The BUYER has THIRTY DAYS from receipt to
accept. Accepting unwinds the trade and extinguishes liability to that buyer; failing to accept within
thirty days waives the buyer’s right of action for that violation.
▲ 	The thirty days is the buyer’s window to accept. It is not a deadline on the seller to make the offer.
● 	JOINT AND SEVERAL LIABILITY extends beyond the seller to: every person who directly or indirectly
controls the seller · every partner, officer, and director of the seller or of a controlling person · every
employee who materially aided the sale · every broker-dealer or agent who materially aided it.
● 	The REASONABLE CARE defense: a control person, partner, officer, director, or employee is not liable
if they prove they did not know, and in the exercise of reasonable care could not have known, of the
facts giving rise to liability. The burden is on the defendant.
● 	STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS — the EARLIER of three years after the violation or two years after
discovery. Earlier, not later. A buyer can lose the right to sue three years after the sale even if they
discovered the problem yesterday.
◆ 	A cause of action survives the death of either party. The buyer’s estate may pursue it; a deceased
seller’s estate may be pursued. Liability attaches to the transaction, not to the lifespan of the people
involved — which is also why a surety bond must remain available for the full limitations period.

◆ 	Criminal liability requires a WILLFUL violation, and the charge is brought by a prosecutor — the state
attorney general or a local prosecutor — not by the Administrator, who may only refer the matter.
● 	Penalties: a fine of up to $5,000, imprisonment for up to 3 years, or both. Multiple violations may be
charged separately.
● 	Statute of limitations for criminal prosecution: 5 years after the alleged violation.

◆ 	What “willful” means here is narrower than intuition suggests. It means the act was DELIBERATE —
the person meant to do the thing they did. It does NOT require that they knew the act violated the
law. Ignorance of the statute is not a defense.
● 	One narrow defense exists: a person prosecuted for violating a RULE or ORDER may not be
imprisoned if they prove they had no actual knowledge of it. The defense covers rules and orders
only, never the statutory provisions of the Act itself.
◆ 	JUDICIAL REVIEW — any final order of the Administrator may be reviewed by a state court. The court
reviews on the administrative record; new evidence is generally not admitted.
● 	The petition must be filed within SIXTY DAYS after entry of the final order. Filing does NOT
automatically stay the order; a stay must be specifically requested from the court.
● 	Standards of review: findings of FACT are upheld if supported by substantial evidence. Conclusions of
LAW are reviewed de novo. Discretionary determinations are reviewed for abuse of discretion.
▲ 	“Willful means the defendant knew the conduct was illegal.” Wrong — it means the conduct was
intentional.

On calibration
Practice scores are a measurement, and measurements have bias. If a form feels easy, treat its score as
five to eight points higher than your true ability; if a form feels punishing, treat it as roughly honest.
Going into August 10, the number that should make you comfortable is 85% on a form you have not seen
before — not 75%. That gap is the whole lesson from July, and it is cheaper to over-correct for it now
than to discover it again in a testing center.
The one thing to carry in
This exam is a vocabulary test wearing a law-exam costume. Almost every question is decided by
whether you know precisely what a single word means — person, sale, offer, guaranteed, custody,
discretion, assignment, place of business, non-issuer, willful. When a question feels hard, the move is not
to reason harder about the scenario. It is to find the defined term in the stem and ask what the Act says it
means.
